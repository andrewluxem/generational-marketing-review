## Quick scan

### Verdict
**Insufficient evidence.** There is no campaign brief, creative, copy, plan, channel plan, offer, or data to review. A fit judgment is not possible from a one-paragraph request.

### Confidence
**High** — not high confidence that the campaign works or fails, but high confidence that the correct outcome is "insufficient evidence." What was shared is unambiguously not enough to assess audience fit.

### Three strengths *(of the request/approach — not of any campaign, which was not provided)*
1. **Sound sequencing instinct.** *(Observed feature)* Seeking an external read *before* further investment is the right time to check fit, while changes are cheap.
2. **A starting audience lens exists.** *(Observed feature)* A cohort is named ("Gen Z"), which gives us one lens to build on — provided it is defined rather than treated as a finished persona.
3. **A clear decision is stated.** *(Provided fact)* They want a go/no-go fit judgment plus recommended next steps, so the deliverable is well scoped once inputs arrive.

### Three risks
1. **Nothing to evaluate.** *(Observed feature → Unknown)* With no artifacts, any verdict on "will it work" would be fabricated. Confidence must stay low until material is shared.
2. **"Gen Z" is undefined and could invite stereotyping.** *(Unknown)* No birth-year/life-stage window, geography, or language was given. Per the skill, an unqualified cohort label is not a persona and not proof of behavior; I will not attach uncited birth ranges, platform rankings, or behavioral "facts" to it.
3. **No objective, customer state, channel, offer, category, or success metric.** *(Unknown)* Fit depends far more on customer need, moment, and conversion action than on age alone. Without these, the rubric's core dimensions (need/context, value prop, channel fit, offer friction, measurement) cannot be scored above 0–1.

### Three priority changes (do these before any review)
1. **Send the actual campaign.** Brief and objective; creative and copy; channel/media plan; offer, price, and any discounts; landing page and checkout/fulfillment flow; and any audience research with its source and date.
2. **Define the audience precisely.** Birth-year or life-stage window, geography, language, product/service category, and customer journey stage — plus the source of any audience claims.
3. **State the success metric and guardrails.** The primary metric, baseline, and what "working" means, so the review can end in a testable recommendation rather than an opinion.

### Confidence and missing evidence
Confidence in this verdict is **high**; confidence about the campaign itself is **not applicable** — it is unseen.

Required context still missing (from the skill's checklist):
- target cohort **definition** (birth-year/life-stage), geography, and language;
- product/service category;
- customer state / journey stage;
- campaign objective;
- channel and placement;
- asset format;
- offer, price, delivery, returns, fulfillment constraints;
- brand voice and accessibility requirements;
- source of any audience claims;
- success metric.

**Recommended next step:** *Gather more evidence.* I'd suggest a short intake request for the items above. Once the campaign and an audience definition arrive, I can run a full marketing-plan (or asset) review with a scored rubric, stereotype/evidence/freshness flags, prioritized fixes, and a test plan. If they genuinely cannot share materials, I can instead deliver a Gen Z **fit checklist and test plan** they can apply themselves — framed as hypotheses to validate, not assumptions to trust.
