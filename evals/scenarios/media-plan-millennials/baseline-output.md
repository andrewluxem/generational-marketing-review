## Verdict

As written, this plan is likely to **hit its vanity metric and miss its business objective.** It is optimized to buy 120M impressions, but the money is supposed to buy 40,000 customers at ≤$28 CAC and $2.0M in revenue — and almost nothing in the channel mix, metric set, or budget is actually pointed at those outcomes. The single most valuable asset in the document (the refill email flow) is buried at 3% funding and excluded from the scorecard, even though the unit economics literally can't work without it.

The good news: the fixes are mostly reallocation and reframing, not a teardown.

## The core disconnect (fix this first)

**The success metric measures the wrong thing.** Primary KPI is total impressions; revenue, customers, and CAC live in a different section and aren't tracked as success criteria. There is no holdout or incrementality design, so the plan can't even tell whether impressions *caused* sales. You will be able to report "we delivered 120M impressions" while having no defensible read on whether the $900k worked. Swap the headline: **primary = first-time customers and blended CAC; secondary = revenue, refill attach rate, subscription-start rate, retention.** Impressions become a diagnostic, not the goal.

**The revenue target secretly depends on refills — which nobody is measuring.** Do the arithmetic: 40,000 first-time customers × $24 Starter Kit = **$960k**, less than half the $2.0M goal. To reach $2.0M each customer must generate ~$50 within six months — roughly a kit *plus* two refill packs or a subscription. So the revenue objective is mathematically a **refill-and-retention objective**, yet repeat purchase, Refill Club retention, and LTV are explicitly not measured, and the email flow that drives them is funded only as "tooling." The margin note already admits first orders are break-even-to-loss; profit is 100% downstream of behavior the plan neither funds nor tracks.

**The CAC target is fragile given the mix.** $900k / 40k = $22.50 per customer if *every* dollar and *every* customer converted perfectly — technically under $28, with almost no cushion. But 70% is on awareness-style Instagram plus macro-influencer reach for a **zero-awareness brand asking for an unfamiliar behavior.** That mix does not realistically convert at $22.50 blended. The break-even-or-loss first order plus a thin CAC cushion plus an awareness-heavy plan is how you overspend to CAC and still miss volume.

## Message and audience: the load-bearing assumption is unsupported

The whole creative rests on **"Millennials prioritize sustainability above price and convenience"** — with no source, no first-party data, and, notably, **contradicted by the plan's own customer-state section**, which says most of these people currently buy conventional single-use bottles. Revealed behavior says price and convenience are winning today. Treating ~65M people born across 15 years as one audience with one motivation compounds the risk.

Separately, the sustainability story is **claimed, not proven**: "eco-friendly," "planet-first," "less plastic," with no lifecycle data, no certification, no ingredient disclosure, and no figure for plastic actually avoided. Against a mildly skeptical audience that's weak persuasion, and under FTC Green Guides, vague green claims are a compliance exposure, not just a soft sell.

The real barrier isn't awareness of sustainability — it's **friction and uncertainty about the refill behavior** ("do I really drop a pod in tap water and trust it works?"). A feel-good awareness message doesn't reduce that friction. The provable, behavior-relevant benefits — cost-per-bottle savings, convenience, less clutter, "one kit replaces X bottles a year" — are stronger hooks, and they're exactly what the (excellent) email flow already leans on. Sustainability should be a supporting reason-to-believe, not the sole bet resting on an unproven premise.

## Channel and creator problems

- **Instagram doing all three funnel jobs at 70%** is single-platform concentration risk, and one awareness-native surface rarely does awareness *and* efficient conversion well.
- **No search** is the clearest miss. The plan concedes there are high-intent queries ("refillable cleaning spray," "plastic-free all-purpose cleaner"). That's cheap, ready-to-convert demand — the fastest path to a defensible CAC — and it's excluded while break-even first orders are being bought elsewhere. Add branded + high-intent non-brand search.
- **Creator strategy is optimized for reach, not trust or conversion.** 12 macro creators, one post each, chosen on "largest reach per dollar," success = follower reach. For a trust-and-behavior-change product you want *demonstration* and credibility: fewer macro, more mid/micro creators who genuinely use refillables, multi-touch or how-to content, and success measured on conversions/assisted conversions, not raw reach. And the brief omits **FTC #ad disclosure entirely**, which is a stated regulatory constraint — that's a compliance gap, not an oversight to fix later.
- **Accessibility standard isn't applied to social/creator/YouTube.** The brand requires captions on video and alt text on images; the plan only applies this to email. Captions on Reels/bumpers are both compliance *and* reach (sound-off viewing).

## What's genuinely good (keep and elevate)

- **The onboarding/refill email flow is the best thing in the plan** — concrete, on-brand (plain, non-preachy), accessible (alt text, high contrast), transparent on cost and cancellation. It's also the exact mechanism that drives the refills the revenue math requires. It should be a **funded, measured growth pillar with its own targets** (open→reorder rate, Refill Club start rate, 90/180-day retention), not a 3% tooling line.
- Budget is itemized, adds up, and includes a contingency line.
- The conversion event is clearly defined, and the margin note is honest about break-even.

## What to change or test

1. **Reframe KPIs** to customers, CAC, revenue, refill attach, subscription-start, and retention. Demote impressions to diagnostic.
2. **Fund and measure retention.** Move email/CRM out of "tooling" into a real line with targets; the profit engine lives here.
3. **Add search** (branded + high-intent generic) to capture existing demand and lower blended CAC.
4. **Rebalance off 70% single-platform** toward conversion + retention; give Instagram a defined funnel role rather than all three.
5. **Rebuild the creator brief:** category credibility and demonstration over raw reach, mandatory FTC disclosure, conversion-based success.
6. **A/B the message assumption** instead of asserting it: sustainability-led vs. value/convenience-led vs. combined. Let the data decide; revealed behavior suggests value may win.
7. **Substantiate or soften green claims** — quantify plastic avoided, add ingredient transparency or a cert (Green Guides risk otherwise).
8. **Add a lightweight incrementality read** — a geo holdout or matched-market test is cheap insurance on a $900k launch.
9. **Apply the accessibility standard everywhere** (captions on Reels/bumpers, alt text on social).
10. **Sanity-check feasibility in-model:** with 40k customers needing ~$50 each to reach $2.0M, either the customer target, the AOV/refill assumption, or the revenue target needs to be reconciled before launch.

The four-week timeline is achievable, but not for 12 vetted, disclosure-compliant macro partnerships plus new creative — so use the reframe to start narrower (search + a demonstration-led creator test + the email flow) and scale the winners.
