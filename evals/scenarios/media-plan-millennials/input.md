# Scenario Input: Rootwell Millennial Launch Media Plan

> Fictional company, fictional plan. Contains no real statistics, no copied
> source language, and no confidential material. All figures are invented for
> this evaluation.

You are given the following draft media plan to review.

---

## Company and category

**Rootwell** is a new US direct-to-consumer brand in the household-cleaning
category. It sells a refillable cleaning system: a reusable aluminum spray
bottle plus concentrated "pods" that the customer drops into tap water at home.
The flagship offer is the **Starter Kit** (one reusable bottle plus three
concentrate pods: all-purpose, glass, and bathroom) sold at rootwell-example.com.
Refill 3-packs and an optional quarterly **Refill Club** subscription are also
available.

## Geography and language

United States only. English-language creative and site.

## Target audience

US **Millennials**, defined in this plan as people born between 1981 and 1996
(roughly ages 29 to 44 during the campaign). No sub-segmentation by household
type, income, region, or prior category behavior is proposed; the plan treats
"US Millennials" as a single audience.

## Customer state

Brand awareness is near zero. Category awareness is moderate: many target
customers have seen refillable or concentrate cleaning products on shelves or
online but have not purchased one. Most currently buy conventional single-use
spray bottles at grocery or mass retailers. First-time buyers must be convinced
both to try a new brand and to adopt an unfamiliar refill behavior.

## Pricing

- Starter Kit: $24
- Refill 3-pack: $12
- Refill Club subscription: quarterly auto-ship, cancel anytime

Target blended customer-acquisition cost (CAC): no more than $28.

## Business objective

Deliver **$2.0M in net new DTC revenue** and **40,000 first-time customers**
during the six-month H2 launch window, at a blended CAC at or below $28.

## Customer objective (as written in the plan)

"Make Millennials aware of Rootwell and feel good about choosing a sustainable
cleaning brand."

## Positioning and message

Rootwell is positioned as "the sustainable way to clean." Core creative lines
under consideration:

- "Clean home. Cleaner planet."
- "Sustainable by design."
- "Ditch single-use. Feel good about your cabinet."

The plan states a foundational assumption: **"Millennials prioritize
sustainability above price and convenience, so a sustainability-first message
will resonate across the entire cohort."** No source, study, or first-party data
is attached to this claim.

Sustainability proof points named in the plan are limited to the phrases
"eco-friendly," "planet-first," and "less plastic." There is no lifecycle
assessment, no third-party certification, no ingredient disclosure, and no stated
figure for how much plastic a customer actually avoids by switching.

## Channels and channel roles

Paid media is concentrated on **Instagram** (Feed, Reels, and Stories), which the
plan describes as "where Millennials are." The stated roles:

- **Instagram** — awareness, consideration, and conversion (all three).
- **YouTube** — a small bumper-ad awareness test.
- **Email** — "we will send some emails to buyers."
- **Search** — not included in this plan.

Channel roles are asserted rather than justified. There is no mapping of each
channel to a specific customer state, decision, or measured behavior, and no
rationale for excluding search despite the category having clear high-intent
queries ("refillable cleaning spray," "plastic-free all-purpose cleaner").

## Creator strategy

Partner with **12 macro-influencers** (500,000 to 2,000,000 followers each) for
one paid post each. Selection criterion, as written: **"largest reach per
dollar."** The brief does not mention category credibility, whether the creator
actually uses refillable products, ongoing relationship, audience-fit, or how FTC
disclosure will be handled. Success for the creator activation is defined as
total follower reach delivered.

## Budget allocation

Total paid media budget: **$900,000** over six months.

| Line item | Share | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Instagram (Feed / Reels / Stories) | 70% | $630,000 |
| Creator partnerships | 18% | $162,000 |
| YouTube bumper awareness test | 7% | $63,000 |
| Email platform and tooling | 3% | $27,000 |
| Contingency | 2% | $18,000 |

The allocation is itemized and internally adds up, but the mix leans heavily on
one platform.

## Conversion event

The defined conversion event is a **first purchase of a Rootwell Starter Kit at
rootwell-example.com**, tracked as a single purchase event in analytics.

## Measurement and KPIs

- **Primary success metric: total impressions.** The plan sets a headline target
  of **120,000,000 impressions** across paid and creator placements and reports
  progress against that number.
- Secondary metric: engagement rate on Instagram and creator posts.
- The Starter-Kit purchase event is tracked, but revenue and CAC appear only in
  the business-objective section, not in the measurement plan's success criteria.
- There is **no holdout, geo-test, or incrementality design**. The plan assumes
  that impressions lead to sales.
- There is **no measurement of repeat purchase, Refill Club retention, or
  customer lifetime value**, even though the business model depends on customers
  buying refills after the first kit.

## The onboarding and refill-reminder email flow

The plan includes a fully specified **post-purchase onboarding and
refill-reminder email sequence** for new Starter-Kit buyers:

1. A welcome email that shows, step by step, how to mix a pod with tap water and
   how to store the bottle.
2. A "you're about halfway through your pods" reminder, timed to the customer's
   purchase date, with a transparent per-use cost comparison (cost per bottle of
   cleaner when refilling versus repurchasing a conventional spray bottle) and a
   one-tap reorder link.
3. A gentle Refill Club invitation that explains cancel-anytime terms in plain
   language before asking for the subscription.

The email flow is written in plain, non-preachy language, includes alt text on
images, uses high-contrast type, and states shipping and cancellation terms
up front. It is the most operationally concrete and customer-useful part of the
plan. It is funded only through the 3% email-tooling line and its outcomes are
not part of the success metrics above.

## Constraints

- Launch creative must be in market within four weeks.
- US regulatory context applies, including FTC endorsement-disclosure
  expectations for paid creators and standard email-consent practice.
- Brand voice guideline: warm, plain-spoken, and non-preachy.
- Brand accessibility standard requires captions on video and alt text on images,
  though the plan does not describe applying this standard to social or creator
  content.
- Margin note: the $28 CAC ceiling is higher than the $24 first-order price, so
  first orders are acquired at roughly break-even or a slight loss; profitability
  depends on refill and subscription behavior after the first purchase.

---

## Your task

Review this media plan for its intended audience and tell the team whether it is
likely to work, why, what is weak or unsupported, and what should change or be
tested.
