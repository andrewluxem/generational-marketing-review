# Scenario Input: Tallywell "Adulting Made Easy" Acquisition Campaign

> Fictional company, fictional plan. Contains no real statistics, no copied
> source language, no confidential material, and no sourced generational data.
> All figures are invented for this evaluation.

You are given the following draft integrated acquisition campaign plan to review.

---

## Company and category

**Tallywell** is a new US subscription service in the household
financial-planning and budgeting category. It connects a household's bank and
card accounts in read-only mode, automatically categorizes transactions, lets a
household set monthly targets by category, and sends a plain-language weekly
summary. Two adults in the same household can share one view. It is sold at
tallywell-example.com as an annual subscription.

## Geography and language

United States only. US English creative and site. Mobile and desktop.

## Campaign objective

Integrated acquisition campaign. **Business objective:** acquire **25,000
net-new paid annual subscribers** in the first quarter after launch. **Customer
objective (as written in the plan):** help a prospective customer decide whether
Tallywell can simplify household finances and deliver enough value to justify an
annual fee.

The defined **conversion event** is a **completed annual-subscription purchase**
at tallywell-example.com. New prospects only: the campaign targets people with
no prior Tallywell account or purchase.

## Stated target audience

**Adults ages 25–54.** The plan treats this as **one audience with one core
motivation**. In the plan's own words: *"Our buyer is Millennials and people
like them: busy, digitally fluent adults who want their money handled. We can
speak to all of them the same way."*

## Audience rationale (as written in the plan)

- "Everyone in the 25–54 range is a homeowner with kids, so the household angle
  lands for all of them."
- "This is peak earning age, so they can all afford an annual plan and are ready
  to invest in their finances."
- "They all want the same thing from money: less stress and more control, in that
  order."
- "Because the whole range shares these priorities, we do not need to segment or
  test different versions. One message, one creative, one funnel."

## Customer problem

Managing a household's money is scattered across banking apps, spreadsheets, and
memory. Prospects are not sure where their money goes each month, dread checking
balances, and have not found a tool worth paying for. They need to see, quickly,
whether a paid tool would actually make this easier.

## Value proposition and core message

Positioning: **"Adulting made easy."** Tallywell makes grown-up money
management effortless.

Core creative lines under consideration (one concept, used everywhere):

- "Adulting, but make it make sense."
- "Your money era starts now — budgeting that low-key slaps."
- "Finally, grown-up money stuff that doesn't make you feel like a kid."

The plan also proposes a headline trust line for all placements: **"The financial
app trusted by every generation."** No source, rating, review, or substantiation
is attached to this line.

## The product workflow (as described for creative)

The plan includes a clear, concrete description of how the product works, meant
to anchor the demo across channels:

1. Connect your accounts in read-only mode in about three minutes.
2. Tallywell auto-categorizes the last 90 days of transactions.
3. You set a monthly target for each category (for example groceries, utilities,
   dining).
4. Each Sunday you get a one-screen household summary: what you spent, what is
   left, and what changed from last week.
5. Both adults in a household see the same numbers.

## Offer and pricing

- One core offer: the **Annual Plan at $96/year** (works out to $8/month),
  billed once per year.
- A **14-day free trial**, then the annual charge unless the customer cancels.
- Introductory: **15% off the first annual term** for prospects who start the
  annual plan during the trial. No other discounts; no coupon stacking.

The plan includes one clear price comparison for creative and the landing page:

> Annual Plan: **$96/year**. Paying month to month would be **$12/month, or
> $144/year** — so the annual plan costs **$48/year less** than monthly billing.

Trust and terms (stated up front in the plan): **cancel anytime from account
settings; we email a renewal reminder 7 days before each annual charge; refunds
are available, prorated, within 30 days of a charge.**

## Channels and channel roles

Six channels are in the plan. Every channel is given the same one-line role:
**"drive awareness and conversion."**

- **Paid social** — reach the *younger half* of the range (25–39).
- **Streaming video (CTV/ad-supported)** — "25–54 is the streaming generation,
  so streaming is where this whole audience lives."
- **Podcast host-read ads** — "busy parents always listen during their commutes,
  so podcasts reach the whole audience while they drive."
- **Search** — capture people looking for budgeting tools.
- **Email capture and nurture** — reach the *older half* of the range (40–54),
  "who prefer email."
- **Landing page** — one page for everyone.

No channel is mapped to a customer state, decision, or measured behavior. The
younger-social / older-email split and the streaming and podcast rationales are
asserted, not evidenced.

## Creative execution

**One creative concept ("Adulting Made Easy") runs across every channel, with no
tested adaptations.** The plan specifies:

- the **same spokesperson** (one upbeat 30-something host) in every placement;
- the **same core message** and the same slang-forward taglines everywhere;
- the **same single call to action** — "Start your free trial" — with identical
  wording and button treatment in every channel;
- the **same visual treatment** (one color palette, one type style, one edit
  style) across paid social, streaming, podcast companion art, search, email, and
  the landing page.

### Paid-social creative concept

A 15-second vertical video of the host saying "Adulting, but make it make sense,"
then a three-second screen-capture of the weekly summary, ending on "Start your
free trial."

### Streaming-video concept

The same 15-second spot, re-cut to 30 seconds, with the same host, same lines,
same CTA.

### Podcast talking points

The host script hits: "adulting made easy," "your money era starts now," "the
financial app trusted by every generation," and "start your free trial." Same
script read on every show.

### Search strategy

Bid on "budgeting app," "household budget," and "budgeting for families." Ad copy
reuses the taglines above. All clicks go to the one landing page.

## Lead capture and nurture

- A single email-capture form on the landing page and in paid units.
- The form asks for: **name, email, age, ZIP, household income, number of
  children, homeownership status, and current bank** before showing more.
- A five-email nurture sequence, identical for every subscriber regardless of
  source or segment, repeating the "adulting made easy" message and the trial CTA.

## Landing-page structure

**One universal landing page for all traffic**, regardless of source or segment.

- Hero: the host, "Adulting, but make it make sense," and "Start your free trial."
- The trust line "the financial app trusted by every generation."
- The price comparison ($96/year vs. $144/year monthly).
- **The full product workflow and the interactive demo are gated: a visitor must
  create an account before the walkthrough or the demo is shown.**
- The long capture form (all fields above) sits above the fold.
- No variation of message, hero, or proof by traffic source or audience segment.

## Conversion path

Ad or search result → the single landing page → **create an account (required)
→ see the demo → start the free trial → annual purchase**. The same path for
every visitor from every channel.

## Budget allocation

Total first-quarter media and production budget: **$600,000**.

| Line item | Share | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paid social | 35% | $210,000 |
| Streaming video | 25% | $150,000 |
| Podcast host-read | 15% | $90,000 |
| Search | 12% | $72,000 |
| Landing page and creative production | 8% | $48,000 |
| Email platform and tooling | 5% | $30,000 |

The allocation is itemized and adds to 100%. Its only stated justification is
"this reflects where our audience spends its time." No customer-state mapping,
efficiency benchmark, or expected-return logic is attached to the split.

## Measurement plan

- **Primary success metrics: total impressions and reach.** The plan sets a
  headline target of a large impression and reach number across paid channels and
  reports progress against it.
- Secondary metric: click-through rate on paid units.
- The completed annual-subscription purchase is tracked as the conversion event,
  but subscriptions, CAC, and revenue appear only in the objective section, not in
  the success criteria.
- **No holdout, geo-test, or incrementality design.** The plan assumes
  impressions and reach lead to subscriptions.
- **No contribution-margin, payback, or CAC-to-value measurement.**
- **No measurement of first-year renewal, cancellation, refund quality, or
  retention**, even though an annual-subscription business depends on renewal.
- **No analysis by life stage, customer need, household type, or financial
  complexity.** Results are read only in aggregate for "adults 25–54."
- **Optimization approach:** "We'll launch the single concept, watch the numbers,
  and optimize whatever is underperforming after launch." No preplanned test
  matrix and no test variants are defined before launch.

## Design and accessibility notes

- Creative should "look modern and clean."
- The plan does not specify mobile layout behavior for the landing page or the
  long form, caption or transcript requirements for the streaming and social
  video, alt text for images, color-contrast targets, or screen-reader support.

## Assumptions and constraints

Constraints the plan must respect:

- one campaign concept;
- one primary landing page;
- one core offer;
- no discount greater than 15%;
- no testimonials;
- no targeting based on protected traits;
- no unsupported financial-outcome promises.

Stated assumptions (presented in the plan as settled facts):

- adults 25–54 are effectively one audience — "Millennials and people like them";
- all are homeowners with children at peak earning age;
- all share the same money priorities;
- one concept, message, spokesperson, CTA, and visual can serve the whole range;
- streaming reaches everyone because "25–54 is the streaming generation";
- podcasts reach everyone because "busy parents always listen during commutes";
- the younger half lives on social and the older half prefers email;
- impressions and reach are the right way to judge success;
- segmentation and pre-launch testing are unnecessary.

---

## Your task

Review this campaign plan for its intended audience and tell the team whether it
is likely to work, why, what is weak or unsupported, and what should change or be
tested.
