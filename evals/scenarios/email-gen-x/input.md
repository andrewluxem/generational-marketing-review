# Scenario Input: Hearthwell Home Gen X Subscription Lifecycle Email

> Fictional company, fictional email. Contains no real statistics, no copied
> source language, no real campaign material, and no confidential information.
> All names, figures, and terms are invented for this evaluation.

You are given the following draft lifecycle email to review.

---

## Company and category

**Hearthwell Home** is a US home-maintenance service. It sends licensed
technicians to a customer's home to perform routine upkeep — the recurring
maintenance tasks most homeowners forget between emergencies. It sells two
things:

- a one-time **Seasonal Tune-Up** visit, bought à la carte; and
- the **Hearthwell Annual** subscription: four scheduled seasonal visits a year
  that cover a fixed 12-task maintenance checklist (for example: swapping HVAC
  filters, flushing the water heater, testing smoke and carbon-monoxide
  detectors, clearing gutters, and checking exterior caulking and weather
  stripping).

Everything is sold and managed at hearthwell-example.com.

## Geography and language

United States only. English-language creative and site.

## Target audience

US **Gen X**, defined in this plan as people born between 1965 and 1980
(roughly ages 45 to 60 during the campaign). The plan treats "Gen X homeowners"
as a single audience and does not sub-segment by home type, tenure, income,
region, or the specific tune-up service the customer previously bought.

## Customer state

Every recipient is a **prior one-time customer**: each person bought a single
Seasonal Tune-Up visit **9 to 15 months ago** and has **not** started an Annual
subscription since. They have first-hand experience of one Hearthwell visit,
a payment relationship, and an email address collected at that purchase. They
are not new leads and they are not current subscribers.

## Business objective

Convert prior one-time purchasers into **paying Hearthwell Annual subscribers**
during a one-month lifecycle push.

## Customer objective (as understood by the team)

Let the customer decide whether an Annual subscription actually **saves them
meaningful time or money** versus continuing to book visits one at a time (or
doing the tasks themselves).

## Channel and device

- **Channel:** a single promotional email. No other channel is used in this
  push.
- **Device:** the email must work on **both desktop and mobile**. Recipients
  open on phones, tablets, and laptops.

## Offer and pricing

- **Hearthwell Annual:** $348 per year (billed annually; equivalent to $29 a
  month) for four seasonal visits covering the 12-task checklist.
- **À la carte reference:** a single Seasonal Tune-Up visit is about $135, so
  four comparable visits bought one at a time run roughly $540 a year.
- **This-month incentive:** start an Annual plan this month and take **15% off
  the first year** (bringing the first year to about $296). No larger discount
  is offered.
- **Operational terms:** cancel anytime in two clicks, refunded prorated to the
  day; Hearthwell emails a reminder seven days before every scheduled visit.

## Constraints

- **One email only.** This lifecycle push is a single send.
- **No discount larger than 15%.**
- **No invented or unverifiable customer testimonials.** The team may not quote
  named customers or fabricate reviews.
- US regulatory context applies, including standard commercial-email consent and
  unsubscribe practice (CAN-SPAM) and truthful-advertising expectations.
- Brand voice guideline: warm, plain-spoken, and respectful of the customer's
  time and intelligence.

## Conversion event

The single defined conversion event is a **completed Hearthwell Annual
subscription purchase at hearthwell-example.com**, tracked as one subscription
purchase event in analytics.

## Measurement plan (as written)

- The only tracked outcome is the **completed annual-subscription purchase
  event** described above.
- There is **no holdout or control group**, so the push cannot show whether it
  drove subscriptions that would not have happened anyway.
- There is **no incrementality or matched-audience design**.
- There is **no measure of first-year renewal or ongoing retention**, even
  though an annual subscription only pays back over multiple years.
- There is **no measure of unsubscribe quality or list health** — no tracking of
  unsubscribes, spam complaints, or whether the send burns future contactability
  with this already-earned audience.

## Team assumptions and notes (as written in the plan)

- The plan states a foundational channel assumption: **"Gen X prefers email over
  every other channel, so a single email is the right and sufficient way to
  reach this audience."** No source, first-party open/click data, or test is
  attached to this claim, and no other channel is even considered.
- The plan describes the tone goal as "make them feel understood as a
  generation."
- The plan assumes the subject line should "lead with nostalgia" because "that
  is what resonates with this age group."

## The email artifact

The full draft email is specified below.

- **From name:** Marcus at Hearthwell Home
- **Subject line:** Remember when you could just trust a handshake?
- **Preview text:** The way taking care of a home used to feel.
- **Headline (top of email):** You've earned a home that takes care of itself.

**Body copy (verbatim draft):**

> There was a time when you knew the person who fixed your furnace. You grew up
> in a world where a job was done right because someone shook your hand and meant
> it, where you didn't need an app for every little thing and honestly you were
> better for it. Life was simpler then. You've spent decades learning to do
> things yourself, to not call someone every time something rattles, because
> that is just how your generation was raised — capable, self-reliant, and a
> little suspicious of anyone selling convenience. We get it. You're not the type
> to hand your house over to a subscription. So we'll be quick about it, because
> we respect that you have better things to do than read a long email.
>
> Here's the practical part. Hearthwell Annual sends a licensed technician to
> your home four times a year to handle the twelve maintenance jobs most people
> forget until they turn into an expensive emergency: we swap your HVAC filters,
> flush the water heater, test every smoke and carbon-monoxide detector, clear
> the gutters, and check the caulking and weather stripping before the season
> turns. You get the same checklist every visit, and we email you a reminder
> seven days before we come.
>
> **What it costs, plainly:** The Annual plan is $348 a year for four visits.
> Booking those same four visits one at a time runs about $540 a year. So the
> plan saves you roughly $192 a year — and this month, starting your plan takes
> another 15% off your first year.
>
> Hearthwell has been trusted by families for decades. You already let us into
> your home once. Let us keep it running.

- **Primary CTA (single button, near the bottom of the email):** Start my
  annual plan
- **Supporting proof shown in the email:** the line "trusted by families for
  decades" and a mention that technicians are "licensed." No named reviews,
  ratings, counts, certifications, or verifiable evidence are shown.
- **Offer terms (small print, shown as a dense block beneath the button):**
  "Offer valid this calendar month only for prior Seasonal Tune-Up customers in
  the contiguous United States. 15% applies to the first annual term only;
  subsequent terms bill at the then-current standard rate of $348/year.
  Auto-renews annually until cancelled. Cancel anytime in two clicks; refunds
  prorated to the day of cancellation. Visit scheduling subject to technician
  availability in your service area. Twelve-task checklist may vary by home type
  and local code. Not combinable with other offers. Taxes where applicable."
- **Footer / disclaimer:** standard company address block, an unsubscribe link,
  and the sentence "You are receiving this because you are a Hearthwell
  customer."
- **Design and layout notes (from the plan):** desktop-first design, one column,
  a large hero image of a technician at a front door, and the price comparison
  set in a smaller font below the fold. The plan gives no guidance on how the
  email renders, stacks, or reads on a phone, and no guidance on button size,
  font size, or the legibility of the small-print block on a small screen.

---

## Your task

Review this email for its intended audience and tell the team whether it is
likely to work, why, what is weak or unsupported, and what should change or be
tested.
